# WellSling user requirements

What the people who would use WellSling need, written from their side. The product requirements that meet these needs are in WSL-PRS-001 (`03-requirements.md`) and the links are in the traceability matrix WSL-RTM-001 (`03b-traceability.md`). Sources are the users and stakeholders in WSL-PRB-001, Tables 1 to 3 (users, users and needs, stakeholders). These are proposals until a co-design partner reviews them. Method: STANDARDS section 22.

> **Safety:** The first need is that nobody goes down a well. WellSling is an open engineering reference, not certified lifting or rescue equipment.

Table 1. User requirements.

| ID | Category | Title | Need | Priority | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UR-001 | Safety | Nobody goes down | We can get the animal out without anyone climbing into the well at any stage, because the air at the bottom can kill | High | Farmers and livestock owners; families of rescuers (PRB Tables 2 and 3) |
| UR-002 | Performance | A grown cow or buffalo | It lifts a grown cow or buffalo, up to about 600 kg, and a calf | High | Farmers and livestock owners (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-003 | Performance | Deep wells | It works when the animal is as far down as the deepest open wells in our area, about 15 m | High | Village volunteers; animal rescue organisations (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-004 | Deployment and assembly | Wide wells, weak rims | It stands over open wells up to about 3 m across without loading a crumbling or wet rim | High | Village volunteers; rural fire crews (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-005 | Deployment and assembly | Quick to set up | Three or four of us can have it ready to lift within about 20 minutes of reaching the well | High | Village volunteers and dairy cooperative staff (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-006 | Usability | Short briefing | Villagers who have never used it can work it safely after a short briefing at the well | High | Village volunteers; dairy cooperatives (PRB Tables 2 and 3) |
| UR-007 | Safety | Stop and hold | The animal stops and stays where it is whenever we let go | High | Farmers; rescue organisations (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-008 | Safety | Gentle on the animal | The lift spreads the animal's weight so it is not injured or crushed while it hangs | High | Animal rescue organisations; veterinarians (PRB Tables 2 and 3) |
| UR-009 | Safety | Land without swinging | The animal is set down on firm footing without being swung over the rim | High | Rescue organisations; rural fire crews (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-010 | Usability | Human power only | It works by hand, with no power on site, at an effort ordinary people can keep up in turns | High | Farmers; village volunteers (PRB Table 2 and operating environment) |
| UR-011 | Safety | Frame does not tip | The frame stays standing when the animal swings or kicks, or a guide line is pulled | High | Rural fire crews; rescue organisations (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-012 | Safety | Nobody falls in | People working at the rim are kept from falling into the well | High | Village volunteers; families (PRB Tables 2 and 3) |
| UR-013 | Logistics | Two-person pieces | Every piece can be carried by two people across a field; the kit fits a small trailer or pickup | Medium | Village volunteers; rescue vans (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-014 | Cost | Affordable to share | A village, cooperative or rescue group can afford one kit and share it | Medium | Dairy cooperatives; NGOs (PRB Table 3) |
| UR-015 | Environment and durability | Lives outdoors | It survives years of storage in a shed or yard, monsoon rain, mud and dung | Medium | Dairy cooperatives (PRB operating environment) |
| UR-016 | Maintenance | Local repair | A village welder or fabricator can make and repair the steel and timber parts | Medium | Fabricators and workshops (PRB Table 3) |
| UR-017 | Regulatory | Free to copy | The design is open and stays clear of others' patents, so anyone may build it | Low | Open-hardware community; Amish (PRB constraints and design-arounds) |

Categories: deployment and assembly, performance, usability, safety, environment and durability, logistics, maintenance, cost, regulatory. Priorities: High, Medium, Low.
