# WellSling market and viability

WellSling is for villages, dairy cooperatives and animal rescue groups in regions with many open wells, first in north India, who today either send a person down or use people-rated or improvised gear. No product found does the same job: confined-space tripods are rated for people and cost several thousand dollars, and farm cattle lifts assume the animal is lying on open ground, so an open design that a district workshop can build and a cooperative can share fills a real gap. Prices and benchmarks are cited with the date they were checked. Method: STANDARDS section 23.

> **Safety:** A low price must never come from a weaker rig. Every kit built from this design is proof-loaded at 1.5 times the hook load by a competent person before use.

## Open route

- **Who builds it.** A district fabrication workshop with a welder, a drill press and access to a galvanising plant makes the tripod, feet, spreader, yokes and bridge from the drawings and the build plan; the winch, rope, rigging, slings and harnesses are bought to their standards (WSL-REG-001). The first kit would be built with the co-design partner at TRL 4.
- **How it spreads.** Through dairy cooperative unions and animal rescue organisations that already answer well calls, and through veterinary universities and district fire services. One kit per cluster of villages or per rescue van spreads the cost; a cooperative buying winches and rigging for several kits lowers it.
- **What keeps it open.** Hardware under CERN-OHL-S-2.0 and software under MIT (REUSE compliant), plain webbing bands and a separate passer to stay clear of the patents noted in WSL-PRC-001, and documents written for a capable maker with no special tools.
- **Viability.** No commercial model is proposed. Cost is reported against the USD 1,500 value-engineering target (USD 1,593 over); the savings worth trying are in WSL-DEC-001.
